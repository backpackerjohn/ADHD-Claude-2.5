# Product Contest — Judge Panel Verdict

Three independent judge agents scored the three rival theses (`thesis-A.md` Ignition / execution-first, `thesis-B.md` Ember / project-memory-first, `thesis-C.md` Anchor / daily-anchor-first) against the verified evidence base. Each judge read all theses plus the ground-truth research files; none saw the others' verdicts.

## Why Ember won

1. **It stands on the only wedge the adversarial verification endorsed.** `research/verification.md` stamps project re-entry as "CONFIRMED as best wedge — distinct, severe, and underserved"; capture and planning are saturated lanes. (Judges 1 & 3)
2. **Its architecture aligns with lapsing instead of fighting it.** The demanded daily behavior (2-second capture) is the cheapest in the set, triggered by an ever-present internal state (fear of forgetting), and the product's payoff is *created* by absence — "absence is our demo." Against a 3.3% 30-day retention base rate, the product designed for disappearance wins. (Judge 2)
3. **Best failure-mode honesty.** Point-by-point Mem/Napkin steelman; honesty clause on unmeasured re-entry severity; numeric kill thresholds. (all judges)
4. **Most rigorous unit economics.** Arithmetic verified correct and conservative (~$3.04/mo at P90 usage vs the $5.99 ceiling); only thesis to include transcription + embeddings costs and a spend killswitch. Strongest native fit to the founder's three mandated features. (Judge 3)
5. **Only compounding moat.** The memory corpus + classifier corrections raise switching costs over time; Ignition's unfreezer and Anchor's brief are bolt-on-able by incumbents in a quarter. (Judge 3)

## Why the others lost (and what was true in them)

**Ignition (A), 2nd.** Best citation hygiene and reward science (sub-300ms deterministic rewards; shame amnesty); cheapest to run ($2.46/mo verified) and fastest to ship. Fatal issues: the freeze-moment wedge is verification-flagged as *not fully underserved* (Focusmate/Flow Club/Dubbii; Fomi already ships AI body-doubling); the core premise contains a behavioral paradox (a user too frozen to act must initiate app-opening to receive initiation help — pull-based rescue in a population whose impaired function is initiation); little compounds between sessions. Its honest framing is a *recurring rescue tool*, not a daily habit.

**Anchor (C), 3rd.** Best time-blindness science (felt-time engine, capacity dial) and the only thesis making anti-habituation a core mechanic. Fatal issues: it plants its flag in the exact lane verification forbids ("not another planner — saturated"); Tiimo already ships forgiving rollover and AI breakdown, and rivva/Nia already ships overnight auto-planning (unthreat-modeled); the whole value stream flows through one daily push to a notification-blind population with no fallback verb; highest COGS ($3.57) and a $59/yr annual price that is ~break-even on high-usage users after store fees; heaviest v1 (only thesis requiring an external integration for its core moment).

## The judges' convergent finding

All three judges, independently, wrote some version of: *the actual winning product is B's memory substrate + A's execution engine at the moment of action + C's capacity/felt-time mechanics as external triggers.* This synthesis is binding on the final product (see `winner.md`).

## Adversarial notes carried forward to the red team (Phase 11)

- B's daily capture verb competes with Apple Notes muscle memory in a saturated lane — retention rests on capture-relief being intrinsically rewarding. (Judges 1–3)
- Delay-discounting cuts against B's deposit-now/payoff-later arc — the smuggled-in Doorway daily card carries habituation risk it under-treats. (Judge 2)
- Auto-filing misclassification in week one could kill trust in "zero-decision." (Judge 2)
- Ember could become "the most beautifully organized graveyard" — the execution layer must be real, not a garnish. (Judge 2)
- One-month v1 requires explicit scope cuts (no watch app, no web clipper at launch). (Judge 3)
- Import of a 4,000-note graveyard needs a cost cap (~$4–5 one-time per heavy importer). (Judge 3)
- Citation error found and corrected: Judge 1 caught Thesis A mis-linking a teardown quote to Daring Fireball; noted for the evidence ledger.
